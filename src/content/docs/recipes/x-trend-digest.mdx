## A real conversation

In November 2026 we asked a bot running Gemini 3.8 Flash:

```text
Who on X is talking about <a topic>? Find me the five most popular posts.
```

It first sorted the people in the conversation into four camps, then listed five representative posts.

But the first version had no links. Only after "where are the links?" did it add author profiles and product accounts, with a post link for four of the five.

## Make it a weekly routine

Put that lesson into the request and schedule it:

```text title="Request for the bot"
Every Monday at 9:00, find the 5 most-discussed X posts about "<your topic>" from the past 7 days:
- Start with two or three sentences on the main threads or camps;
- For each post: author (with profile link), one-line takeaway, post link;
- Leave out anything you can't link to;
- If nothing is new since last week, reply only "Nothing new this week."
```

Two lines matter most:

- **"Leave out anything you can't link to."** The typical research-bot failure is a polished summary you can't verify. Making links mandatory beats asking afterwards.
- **"Reply only … if nothing is new."** Scheduled jobs must not spam you.

## Model and tools

- Searching and summarizing doesn't need a strong model. Cheap and fast is fine.
- Use **web search or the browser tool, not a computer.** A browser returns page text directly, while a computer has to click and look its way through, which takes more steps. See [why one task can take an hour](/concepts/long-tasks/).
